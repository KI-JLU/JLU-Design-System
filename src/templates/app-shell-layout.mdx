# AppShellLayout

The complete app chrome: a collapsible, branded **left column** (logo,
navigation, pinned footer), a three-region chrome bar above the page content,
and optionally a **second column on the right**. Apps import this template and
fill its slots — the frame is **never** rebuilt in the app.

**One shell, adapted per context.** Every view of an app is this one
`AppShellLayout`; what changes between views is what goes into the slots, not
which frame renders them. And **a central search is the standard fill of the
bar**: every story on this page passes `search`, and so should every app view
that has something to search. The prop stays optional — an app without search
omits it and keeps the bar's geometry (see „The chrome bar" below).

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.Overview} />

```
| left SidePanel column    | main column                                  | right column (optional)  |
| header: logo + toggle    | bar h-16: pageLabel | search | headerActions | header: toggle + title   |
| nav … sidebarFooter      | <main> scrolls on its own                    | content … footer         |
```

## Two contexts, one shell

The stories are modelled on the two call sites of the reference consumer,
JLURAG (`web/src/components/`), with neutral content. Both are the same
component with a different fill; the shared story fixture that renders them is
`src/test/fixtures/app-shell.tsx`.

## Slots & Props

| Slot / Prop | Type | Required | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `logo` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Brand block — in the left column's header row **and** in the narrow top bar. |
| `nav` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Navigation content — `<NavItem>` rows (with `asChild` for router links), a `SidebarPanel` around them, or a whole panel like the workspace's history. Lands in a `<nav aria-label={navLabel}>`; a `SidebarPanel` child brings its own insets. |
| `sidebarFooter` | `ReactNode` | — | Pinned foot of the left column: the user menu. |
| `navLabel` | `string` | — | Accessible name of the `<nav>` landmark **and** of the column around it (default „Hauptnavigation"). |
| `pageLabel` | `ReactNode` | — | Current location — headline-sized and bold, but a `<p>` and **not** a heading, on the left of the bar. **Optional since 0.29.0**: omitted, the template renders **no element** for it. `truncate` since 0.30.0. |
| `search` | `ReactNode` | — | The bar's **centre** (0.30.0) and **the standard fill of every view**: a search field, capped at 28rem and centred on the *bar*, with or without a label. On a short bar it gives way first, down to 8rem (0.44.1). Not rendered below `lg`. |
| `headerActions` | `ReactNode` | — | Chrome controls at the **right** end of the bar — an icon button, a menu, several, or nothing. The template renders no control of its own there (since 0.26.0). Below `lg` this region sits next to the logo in the narrow bar. |
| `leftOpen` | `boolean` | ✅ | Left column expanded (`true`) or the rail (`false`). **Replaces `collapsed` and means the opposite.** |
| `onLeftOpenChange` | `(isOpen: boolean) => void` | ✅ | The toggle was pressed; receives the **requested** state. |
| `collapseLabel` | `string` | — | Name of the toggle while expanded (default „Navigation einklappen"). |
| `expandLabel` | `string` | — | Name of the toggle while collapsed (default „Navigation ausklappen"). |
| `leftWidth` | `number` | — | **0.36.0**: expanded width of the left column in px. Omitted, `AppShell`'s 256 default applies. |
| `leftResize` | `AppShellPanel["resize"]` | — | **0.36.0**: makes the left column draggable (`minWidth`, `maxWidth`, `onWidthChange`, `label` — all or nothing). Omitted, no separator. |
| `rightPanel` | `AppShellPanel` | — | **0.30.0**: second column on the right, passed whole (the shape `AppShell.left`/`.right` take). Omitted, no landmark and no rail. Carries `resize` since 0.36.0. |
| `showRight` | `boolean` | — | **0.37.0**: `false` takes `rightPanel` out of the **desktop** arrangement **without** touching its `isOpen`, and is ignored below `lg`. Forwarded to `AppShell`. Hide a column per view with this instead of dropping `rightPanel` (that destroys its collapse state). |
| `mobileTabs` | `MobilePaneTab[]` | ✅ | **0.30.0**: tabs of the narrow arrangement, each with `pane: "left" \| "main" \| "right"`. |
| `activeMobileTab` | `string` | ✅ | Id of the current tab — the **only** input that decides which area shows below `lg`. |
| `onMobileTabChange` | `(id: string) => void` | ✅ | A tab was tapped. |
| `mobileTabBarLabel` | `string` | ✅ | Accessible name of the tab bar's `navigation` landmark. |
| `children` | `ReactNode` | ✅ | Page content — a content template (`SectionedGridLayout`, `DashboardLayout`, `TableLayout`, `FormLayout`, …) or a `ChatStage`. |

## The chrome bar: three regions

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithCenteredSearch} />

- **`pageLabel` on the left, `search` in the centre, `headerActions` on the
  right.** The two side regions are identical flex children (`flex-1`, basis
  0, since 0.44.1 with the same floor) — exactly that centres the search on
  the **bar**, not on the space the label leaves.
- **Measured for 0.30.0 in Chromium** (1280px window, the story's `play`
  function) — the numbers document that release: bar 256–1200 → centre 728,
  field 504–952 → centre 728, although the label (125.4px) and the
  right-hand control (then a 102px `ThemeToggle`) differed by 23px. Since
  0.37.0 the label starts at 280 instead of 296 (see „The bar's inset"); the
  centre is untouched. Since KI-847 the right-hand control in the story is a
  36px icon button; the `play` function still requires the two sides to
  differ.
- **0.29.0's `mx-auto` recipe is obsolete.** It centred `headerActions`'
  content in the space *after* the label — with a label the field stood off
  centre and moved whenever the page name changed. A search field goes in
  `search`.
- **Without a label the centre is the same** — the overview context has no
  `pageLabel` (its content template renders the page's title), and its field
  sits on the bar's centre; `Overview` asserts it.
- **The bar stays 64px high** — with label, search only, actions only, all,
  and none:

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithoutPageLabelOrActions} />

  That is a contract outside this repo: JustRAG places its toasts at
  `top: 76px` (= 64 + 12) under this edge. A bar that collapsed once the last
  prop was omitted would be a **breaking geometry change** for an unchanged
  call site. This story is the one that opts out of the search convention,
  explicitly (`search={null}`): it pins what an app gets when it omits every
  slot.

### Short bar: the search gives way first (since 0.44.1)

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.NarrowBarKeepsTheLabel} />

- **Until 0.44.0 the side regions gave way first.** The centre was `w-full
  max-w-md min-w-0` and the sides had no floor, so the search kept its full
  28rem and the label and the actions absorbed the whole shortfall. Measured
  in Chromium (JustRAG KI-838, both columns open at 320px, a 560px bar): the
  label's region was **16px**, a back button plus a topic title got **0px** of
  title, and the back button and the gear each reached 4px into the 448px
  field.
- **Since 0.44.1 the order is: spare room, then the search, then the sides.**
  The centre is a 28rem flex basis that shrinks down to an **8rem** floor;
  each side region keeps an **11rem** floor (a 36px icon button plus a ~120px
  truncated title) until the search has reached its own. Only then do the
  side floors shrink with the row, both equally. In the 560px case the title
  keeps 132px and the field is 128px.
- **The search stays on the bar's exact centre** at every width down to a
  208px bar (search floor + gaps + inset), because the two side regions stay
  identical flex items in every step. Narrower than that the sides are 0 and
  the search overflows — not reachable with two open columns on an `lg`
  window.
- **Known limit:** the floor is a length, not the content's own minimum.
  `headerActions` wider than 11rem is not reserved for and reaches toward the
  search on a short bar. A content-derived floor would switch `truncate` off
  for the label (its min-content is the full text) and break the symmetry the
  centring rests on.
- The three stories measure the **workspace context's own bar** (back button
  and title, scoped search, gear); `ChatWorkspace` checks the same no-overlap
  and centring rules at the consumer's default widths. The wide bar is
  unchanged — the search at its 28rem cap, centred:

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WideBarKeepsTheSearchAtItsMaximum} />

- `BarGivesWayInOrder` narrows one shell from a 1024px to a 300px bar and
  asserts the order relationally: 1024 → 900 the field holds its cap, 800 →
  560 the field loses all 240px and the title none, 560 → 480 the field holds
  its floor and the title loses 40px (half of 80).

### The bar's inset (since 0.37.0)

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.BarInsetIsTheColumnGutter} />

- **The bar takes the column gutter: `px-gutter`, 24px.** Until 0.36.0 both
  bars sat in a `Container`, i.e. in the **page measure** (`px-gutter`, from
  `md` `px-margin-page`, centred and capped). The bar is not page content but
  **chrome between two `SidePanel`s**, so it takes the plain column measure.
- **Measured in Chromium** (1280px window, JustRAG's `KbWorkspaceLayout`,
  21.09.2026): the bar's first content 40px from the column edge, the
  column's logo 24px — the visible gap between header and bars.
- **Since 0.39.0 it is the bar's own measure, not a mirror of the column.** A
  `SidePanel`'s `h-16` header row is `px-4` (16px), because its toggle sits on
  the first control of the column body. The two insets are different on
  purpose.
- **The story's oracle is the token, not a number**:
  `BarInsetIsTheColumnGutter` measures `pageLabel.left − aside.right` against
  `--spacing-gutter` from the CSSOM and also pins that the column's header row
  sits on a different measure.

### `headerActions` — the right-hand position belongs to the app

The template renders **no** control of its own there. What goes in is the
app's decision:

- **One element** (the workspace's gear) sits right-aligned.
- **Several** come as a fragment and stand in a row (`gap-2`).
- **Empty** means no box in the DOM — the overview context's case.

**Since 0.26.0 the shell needs no `ThemeProvider`.** Until then the template
rendered a `ThemeToggle` itself and so required the provider — without it,
`useTheme must be used within a ThemeProvider` was thrown at runtime from a
template whose props say nothing about theming. The appearance controls now
belong to the settings window behind the user menu (see „Two contexts, one
shell").

## The left column

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithCollapsibleColumns} />

```tsx
const [leftOpen, setLeftOpen] = useState(true);   // or URL, context, localStorage

<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo}
  nav={nav}
  sidebarFooter={userMenu}
  search={searchField}
  leftOpen={leftOpen}
  onLeftOpenChange={setLeftOpen}
  collapseLabel={t("collapseNavigation")}   // optional, the default is German
  expandLabel={t("expandNavigation")}
  mobileTabs={TABS}
  activeMobileTab={tab}
  onMobileTabChange={setTab}
  mobileTabBarLabel={t("switchArea")}
>
  {page}
</AppShellLayout>
```

- **The column renders its toggle itself**, in the header row on the edge
  that faces the content — the template brings **no** control of its own here
  and opens no slot. The toggle is the only control that survives collapsing,
  and it carries `aria-expanded` + `aria-controls` onto an `id` only
  `SidePanel` knows (`useId`, per mount). A slot could not reach that `id`.
- **The state belongs to the app.** There is deliberately no `defaultOpen`: a
  column width is exactly the kind of value an app stores per user.
- **Collapsed is the rail** (60px, `SIDE_PANEL_RAIL_WIDTH`). The same `nav`
  node moves into it (0.31.0) — `NavItem`s shrink to their icon form, and a
  `nav` that renders differently in the rail reads `useSidebarCollapsed()`
  (the overview's rows drop their `SidebarPanel`, the workspace's history
  becomes a `SidebarRail`).
- **The rail mirrors the expanded column's rows**, so every control keeps its
  height across a collapse:

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.CollapsedRailKeepsVerticalPositions} />

  The story measures the overview context's composition: the rows in a
  `SidebarPanel` with no title, `head` or `nav` when expanded, bare in the
  rail. Such a panel renders no head, and its list carries the same 16px top
  inset as `AppShellLayout`'s own `p-4` nav wrapper and the rail — so bare
  rows and a head-less panel both start 16px below the chrome row. Until
  KI-852 the panel rendered an empty 28px head, and the first row jumped 12px
  on collapse (92px below the column's top expanded, 80px in the rail).
- **Both label props are translatable** — the defaults are German.

## Second column on the right (since 0.30.0)

`rightPanel` takes a whole `AppShellPanel` instead of six flat props: it is
exactly the shape `AppShell` itself takes, so there is no second vocabulary
for the same thing. `content`, `header`, `footer`, `label`, `isOpen`,
`onOpenChange`, `width` (default 256), `expandLabel`, `collapseLabel`,
`collapsedPreview` — and since 0.36.0 `resize`. The workspace context above
(`ChatWorkspace`) is the case: sources on the right, with a `collapsedPreview`
rail of one icon per source.

## Resizable columns (since 0.36.0)

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.WithResizableColumns} />

```tsx
// The widths belong to the app. `usePersistedWidth` is the optional hook
// that keeps them per device — a `useState` does the job just as well.
const [leftWidth, setLeftWidth] = usePersistedWidth("myapp.kb.leftWidth", {
  defaultWidth: 320, minWidth: 150, maxWidth: 600,
});
const [rightWidth, setRightWidth] = usePersistedWidth("myapp.kb.rightWidth", {
  defaultWidth: 320, minWidth: 300, maxWidth: 800,
});

<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo} nav={history} leftOpen={leftOpen} onLeftOpenChange={setLeftOpen}
  search={searchField}
  leftWidth={leftWidth}
  leftResize={{
    minWidth: 150, maxWidth: 600,
    onWidthChange: setLeftWidth,
    label: t("resizeHistory"),
  }}
  rightPanel={{
    ...sources,
    width: rightWidth,
    resize: {
      minWidth: 300, maxWidth: 800,
      onWidthChange: setRightWidth,
      label: t("resizeSources"),
    },
  }}
  mobileTabs={TABS} activeMobileTab={tab} onMobileTabChange={setTab}
  mobileTabBarLabel={t("switchArea")}
>
  {page}
</AppShellLayout>
```

- **It is the library's one `ResizeHandle`** — no second mechanism. Until 0.35.0 the shell had the columns but no handles: an app on
  `AppShellLayout` could not widen its navigation column at all (JustRAG's
  knowledge-base view, KI-94).
- **`resize` is an object, all or nothing.** As four loose optionals „handle
  without bounds" would be expressible — a `separator` without
  `aria-valuemin`/`-valuemax` is no APG splitter any more. `width` stays
  beside it, because it exists without dragging too (and has a default that
  the bounds deliberately do not have: those are the app's decision).
- **The arrow keys are mirrored per side**: `→` widens the *left* column, `←`
  the *right* one. The key moves the separator, `aria-valuenow` reports the
  column. `Home`/`End` are minimum and maximum and therefore **not** mirrored.
- **No handle on a collapsed column** (the rail is a fixed 60px; a separator
  there would report a value with no visible effect) and **none below `lg`**
  (one area fills the screen — there is nothing to drag the column against).
- **Omitted, nothing changes**: no separator, no `id` on the `<aside>`,
  default 256. An unchanged call site renders the same DOM as before 0.36.0.
- **Since 0.38.0 the handle takes no layout width** — the column's edge *is*
  `<main>`'s edge, and the 8px grab zone straddles it
  (`HandleHasNoLayoutWidth` measures it with the browser's hit-testing).

### Storing the width: `usePersistedWidth`

```tsx
import { usePersistedWidth } from "@ki4jlu/design-system";
```

- **The consumer passes the whole key.** The package assigns no namespace: a
  library-owned prefix would collide between the apps that share a device and
  an origin (JustRAG prefixes `justrag.`).
- **The components stay controlled.** The hook is the piece hung into
  `leftWidth` + `leftResize.onWidthChange` — no default in `AppShellPanel`.
  Whoever keeps the width elsewhere (URL, profile, own store) simply does not
  call it.
- **Read once per mount**, written on every set. **Clamped on read**, not on
  write: a stored value can be older than today's bounds, and unclamped the
  column would show a width the handle can never pull back into range.
- **Every storage access is guarded.** Even *reading*
  `window.localStorage` throws in Safari's private mode and with blocked
  cookies. A lost preference must never cost the view — the hook then falls
  back to plain state.
- **`storage` is injectable** — that is what the field is for: the tests run
  against an in-memory `Storage`, and the story fixture passes one per mount,
  so a test run leaves nothing behind.

## Responsive behaviour

- **from `lg`**: left column | main column | right column, each column full
  height, `<main>` scrolls on its own.
- **below `lg`**: top bar (logo + `headerActions`), **one** area,
  `BottomTabBar` — no burger button, no drawer, no dialog. `search` is not
  rendered there: a field capped at 28rem and centred has no centre on a
  390px bar, and an app that needs search on a phone owns that decision.

<Canvas of={AppShellLayoutStories.Mobile} />

- **Why in JavaScript and not with an `lg:` utility.** Below `lg` an area is
  not a narrower version of itself: it fills the screen, it ignores the
  collapse state, and its collapse toggle has to go (a toggle that collapses
  the only visible area would leave an empty screen and destroy the desktop
  setting on the way). None of the three can be expressed as a class on the
  same markup. The app still writes no breakpoint query: which tab shows
  which area is data (`mobileTabs`).
- **No focus trap, no Escape, no scroll lock — and that is not an
  omission.** Those three belong to an **overlay**: content behind a modal
  dialog must not be reachable, and a dialog needs a way out. The tab
  arrangement overlays nothing — the area shown **is** the page. With the
  dialog the requirement disappears instead of staying unmet. What remains is
  the tab bar's contract: a `navigation` landmark with exactly one
  `aria-current="page"`.
- **One `<main>` per arrangement, never two** — and when a *side* column is
  the area shown below `lg`, there is **no** `main` at all: the main column is
  not in the tree then, and wrapping a `complementary` in a `main` would be
  the worse lie.
- **Every node is mounted exactly once.** The old drawer rendered the same
  nav node a second time — an `id` in it existed twice while it was open
  (KI-797, documented in JustRAG's `AppChrome.tsx`). That went with the
  drawer.

## Migration 0.29 → 0.30 (**breaking**)

```tsx
// before
<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo} nav={nav} sidebarFooter={userMenu}
  menuLabel={t("openNavigation")}     // REMOVED — no burger button
  drawerLabel={t("navigation")}       // REMOVED — no drawer
  headerActions={searchField}
  collapsed={collapsed}               // REMOVED
  onCollapsedChange={setCollapsed}    // REMOVED
>

// after
<AppShellLayout
  logo={logo} nav={nav} sidebarFooter={userMenu}
  search={searchField}                // NEW: the bar's centre
  headerActions={actions}             // optional: the app's own chrome controls
  leftOpen={leftOpen}                 // INVERTED: leftOpen === !collapsed
  onLeftOpenChange={setLeftOpen}
  mobileTabs={TABS}                   // NEW, required
  activeMobileTab={tab}               // NEW, required
  onMobileTabChange={setTab}          // NEW, required
  mobileTabBarLabel={t("switchArea")} // NEW, required
  rightPanel={sources}                // NEW, optional
>
```

- `collapsed` → `leftOpen`, **with the opposite meaning**. A mechanical
  rename silently turns the UI inside out.
- Both are **required** now, as are the four tab props: below `lg` the shell
  shows one area, and without tabs the nav column would not be reachable.
- `menuLabel`/`drawerLabel`: delete. There is no replacement; the areas are
  named through `mobileTabs` and `mobileTabBarLabel`.
- Visible: the collapsed column is 60px instead of 80px and shows no icons
  without `collapsedPreview`. The expanded width is unchanged.

## Do's & Don'ts

- ✅ **One `AppShellLayout` for every view of an app** — adapt the slots per
  context (overview, workspace), never switch to a second frame.
- ✅ **Fill `search`** — the central search is the standard; leave it out only
  where a view has nothing to search.
- ✅ Put appearance (colour scheme, contrast, accent, Style) behind the user
  menu's „Einstellungen" (`SettingsDialog` + `AppearanceSettings`), not into
  the bar.
- ✅ Set the active state through `NavItem active` (sets `aria-current="page"`).
- ✅ Hang page content in as a **content** template (`SectionedGridLayout`,
  `DashboardLayout`, `FormLayout`, `TableLayout`, `ChatLayout`) or a
  `ChatStage`.
- ❌ **Not** every template fits: `AuthLayout` stands outside the shell (sign-in
  pages carry no app chrome), and a frame that brings its own chrome must not
  be nested here, or the screen shows two chrome column sets and two
  `<main>`s. A workspace screen is this template with a `rightPanel`, not a
  nested frame. The inventory in DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §4 decides.
- ✅ `headerActions` and `search` take **chrome**: search, small tools, a
  menu. No page content, no forms, no business logic.
- ❌ **No heading in `headerActions` or `search`.** Both are chrome positions
  in the same bar, not a way around the `pageLabel` rule. Pinned in
  `heading-owner.test.tsx` — with filled slots the template still delivers
  **zero** headings.
- ✅ **Omit** `pageLabel` when the content template already renders the page
  title.
- ❌ **No empty `pageLabel` as a placeholder** (`pageLabel={<span />}`, an
  `&nbsp;`). Omit the prop.
- ❌ Do not build your own column or top bar in the app, or re-skin the
  template through `className` — if something is missing, the template is
  extended here.
- ❌ **Do not** compose `AppShell` + `SidePanel` by hand just to get a second
  column or a collapse toggle.
- ❌ **No drag mechanics of your own** beside `leftResize` /
  `rightPanel.resize` (a `pointermove` listener in the app, a handle made of
  your own markup): `ResizeHandle` owns the interaction, the app owns the
  number.
- ❌ **No collapse button of your own** in `logo` or `headerActions`: the
  column renders its toggle itself, with `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`. Two
  toggles would be two truths.
- ❌ No business logic handed in: routing and active state live in the
  injected `NavItem`s, not in the template.
